## York's housing

York has a large stock of Victorian and Edwardian terraced houses in areas such as South Bank, Clementhorpe, the Groves, Leeman Road and Heworth, along with older buildings inside and around the city walls and a large number of listed buildings and conservation areas. Suburbs and villages including Acomb, Dringhouses, Haxby and Huntington add 20th-century semis and newer estates.

Terraced houses bring their own considerations: narrow plans, solid party walls, shared lofts in some older terraces, and cellars that often hold the meter and consumer unit. Many have been rewired once, decades ago, and extended since.

## Landlords and rental properties

York has a large private rented sector, including student housing for the University of York and York St John University. Private landlords in England must have the electrical installation inspected at least every five years. We carry out [EICRs](/services/electrical-testing-eicr/), explain every code, and quote remedial work so you can see exactly what is required and what is recommended.

Houses in multiple occupation (HMOs) carry additional requirements. Check your licence conditions with City of York Council.

## Period and listed buildings

Work in listed buildings needs extra planning: surface or concealed routes agreed in advance, fittings chosen for the building, and as little disturbance to historic fabric as possible. If your home is listed, check with the council before work starts whether consent is needed.
