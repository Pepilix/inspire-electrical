Lighting is the part of an electrical installation people notice every day. It is also the part most often left until too late: by the time someone asks where the lights should go, the ceilings are boarded.

We install everything from a replacement pendant to full architectural schemes with layered lighting, concealed LED, feature lighting and scene control.

## Architectural lighting

Good architectural lighting is about layers: ambient light, task light where you work, accent light on surfaces and objects, and the absence of glare. It relies on details that are decided early:

- **Fitting positions** set out from furniture and joinery layouts, not a grid
- **Recessed LED profiles** in joinery, plinths, shadow gaps and stair treads, which need channels, drivers and access
- **Drivers and transformers** located where they can be reached later
- **Colour temperature and colour rendering** chosen consistently across the scheme
- **Dimming compatibility** between fittings, drivers and dimmers, which is the most common cause of flicker

Our guide to [planning lighting during a renovation](/advice/planning-lighting-during-a-renovation/) covers the decisions to make before first fix.

## Lighting control

Lighting control replaces rows of switches with keypads that recall scenes. A "dinner" scene might set the kitchen pendants low, the island bright and the garden on; "all off" by the front door does what it says.

The Inspire group has installed lighting control in private homes since 2009 through [Inspire Audio Visual](https://www.inspire-av.com/). Where a project includes a control system, we coordinate the electrical installation with them, or with the client's chosen integrator, so that cabling, back boxes and dimmer locations are right first time.

## Everyday lighting work

Not every job is a scheme. We also replace fittings, add downlights, install bathroom and kitchen lighting, fit dimmers properly matched to LED loads, and sort out lighting that flickers, buzzes or fails.
