Most electrical work in a home is not a rewire or a new build. It is an extra double socket behind the television, outside power for the garden, a new circuit for an induction hob, a shower that needs a bigger supply, or moving switches when a wall comes down.

These jobs deserve the same care as large installations. A socket added to an overloaded or poorly earthed circuit is not a good job, however neat the faceplate.

## What we do

- **Additional sockets and switches**, including USB and outdoor outlets
- **New circuits** for hobs, ovens, showers, home offices, garden rooms and workshops
- **Kitchen electrics**: worktop sockets, appliance isolation, under-cabinet lighting, extractor supplies
- **Bathroom electrics**: lighting, extractor fans, shaver points and heated towel rails, positioned to suit the bathroom zones
- **Extensions and loft conversions**: power, lighting, heating controls and data
- **Moving and removing** sockets, switches and lights during renovation

## How a domestic job runs

1. **Tell us what you need.** A short description and a couple of photographs (the consumer unit and the area you want work in) are usually enough to give an initial view.
2. **We check the existing installation.** Before adding to a circuit we confirm it can safely take the extra load and that earthing and protection are adequate. If something needs attention first, we explain why.
3. **Quotation.** You receive a written quotation describing exactly what is included.
4. **Installation.** Cables are routed and concealed properly, floors and finishes protected, and the area left clean.
5. **Testing and paperwork.** Every circuit we touch is tested. You receive the appropriate certificate, and notifiable work is registered with building control.

## Kitchens, bathrooms and Building Regulations

In England, some domestic electrical work must be notified under Part P of the Building Regulations, including new circuits, consumer unit replacements and certain work in rooms with a bath or shower. Our guide to [electrical work and Building Regulations](/advice/electrical-work-and-building-regulations/) explains what is and is not notifiable.
