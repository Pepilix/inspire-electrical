A rewire replaces the cables, accessories and usually the consumer unit throughout a property, or through part of it. Done well, it gives you an installation that will serve the house for decades and that fits the way you live now: more sockets where you need them, lighting that suits each room, and capacity for things that did not exist when the house was last wired.

## Signs a rewire may be due

- Rubber, lead-sheathed or fabric-insulated cables anywhere in the house
- An old fuse box with rewireable fuses, or no RCD protection
- Sockets on skirting boards in a house that has never been rewired
- Scorch marks, warm accessories, or a burning smell
- Breakers that trip repeatedly with no obvious cause
- Not enough sockets, so extension leads have become permanent

None of these on its own proves a full rewire is needed. An [EICR](/services/electrical-testing-eicr/) gives an objective view of the condition of the installation and is the right starting point if you are unsure.

## How we plan a rewire

**Survey first.** We look at the existing installation, the construction of the house (solid walls, suspended or solid floors, ceiling types) and how you want to use each room.

**Agree a layout.** You get a room-by-room plan of sockets, switches, lighting and any additions such as data, heating controls or outdoor power. This is the time to think about where furniture and televisions will go.

**Sequence the work.** In an occupied home we agree which rooms are worked on when, and keep power to the rooms you are using. In an empty property or full renovation, the rewire is coordinated with the builder, plasterer and other trades.

**First fix.** New cables are run, back boxes fitted and routes recorded before walls are made good.

**Second fix.** Accessories, light fittings and the consumer unit are fitted and connected.

**Test, certify, notify.** The whole installation is inspected and tested. You receive an Electrical Installation Certificate, and the work is notified to building control.

## Older and listed houses

Many homes around Harrogate and York are solid-walled Victorian and Edwardian buildings, and some are listed or in conservation areas. Cable routes, accessory positions and the protection of original features need more thought in these houses. If your home is listed, check with the local planning authority whether listed building consent is needed before work starts.
