Lighting is the electrical work you notice every day, and the part most often decided too late. By the time a room is plastered, moving a light means cutting into a finished ceiling. These are the decisions worth making before [first fix](/advice/first-fix-and-second-fix-explained/).

## 1. Think in layers, not grids

A grid of downlights lights the floor evenly and the room badly. Good lighting usually combines:

- **Ambient light** for general use, often indirect: wall lights, coves, uplights
- **Task light** where you do things: worktops, desks, reading chairs, mirrors
- **Accent light** on surfaces and objects: artwork, joinery, stone, planting outside the window
- **Decorative fittings**: pendants and lamps that are part of the interior

Each layer on its own circuit lets you set the room for different times of day.

## 2. Start from the furniture plan

Set out lights from where the table, island, sofa, bed and artwork will be, not from the centre of the ceiling. A pendant over a dining table is only right if the table is where you think it is.

## 3. Check the ceiling before you choose fittings

- **Joist direction and depth** decide where recessed fittings can go.
- **Fire separation**: cutting holes in a ceiling between floors can compromise it. The fitting and method need to maintain it.
- **Insulation**: in a top-floor ceiling, fittings must suit contact with, or clearance from, loft insulation.
- **Acoustic ceilings and lowered sections** may be needed for some details.

## 4. Plan the LED details

Linear LED in joinery, plinths, shelving, stair treads and shadow gaps can transform a space. It needs:

- a recess or profile designed into the joinery
- a driver in a location that can be reached later (not sealed behind a kitchen carcass)
- a cable route from the driver to the tape, and from the driver to the switching

## 5. Make dimming work

Most flicker and buzzing complaints come from mismatched dimmers, drivers and lamps. Choose fittings, drivers and dimmers as a system, and check the dimming protocol (leading edge, trailing edge, DALI, 0-10V or a control system) before ordering.

## 6. Decide on control early

Conventional switching, smart dimmers or a whole-house lighting control system all need different wiring. Lighting control usually means cables from each circuit back to a central location and keypads instead of banks of switches. That decision needs to be made before first fix.

## 7. Keep colour consistent

Pick a colour temperature (often 2700K in living spaces) and stick to it across the scheme. Look for good colour rendering (CRI 90 or above is a common target for interiors) so materials and skin tones look right.

Lighting the garden extends the view from inside at night and removes the black-mirror effect of large glazing. Ducts laid before landscaping make it easy. See [outdoor and garden lighting](/services/outdoor-lighting/).
